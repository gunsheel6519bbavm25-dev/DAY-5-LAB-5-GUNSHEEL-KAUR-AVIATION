# DAY-5-LAB-5-GUNSHEEL-KAUR-AVIATION
Day 5 Generative AI lab on iterative prompting, output diagnosis, targeted prompt refinement, verification safeguards and human review in aviation communication.

Programme: BBA Aviation Management
Course: Generative AI for Business
Lab: Day 5 — Iterative Prompting

Objective

The objective of this lab is to understand iterative prompting and how AI-generated outputs can be improved through a series of specific follow-up instructions.

The process followed in this lab is:

Generate → Evaluate → Diagnose → Repair → Review → Approve

The first AI-generated response should not be accepted automatically. It must be reviewed for factual accuracy, tone, compliance and contextual problems.

Aviation Scenario

The main scenario focuses on Flight AV307 from Chandigarh to Mumbai.

Verified Information
Flight: AV307
Route: Chandigarh to Mumbai
Reason for delay: Adverse weather
Delay: 75 minutes
Revised departure: 7:15 PM
Passenger instruction: Monitor official airport display screens
Compensation policy: Not provided
Gate number: Not provided
Meal or hotel arrangement: Not provided
Initial Prompt

The initial prompt was:

Write an announcement for passengers of Flight AV307 about the delay.

The initial AI output communicated the flight delay, route, reason, revised departure time and passenger instructions.

Output Diagnosis

The initial output was reviewed for different areas:

Flight details
Delay information
Revised departure time
Passenger instructions
Gate information
Compensation or benefits
Tone
Length
Overall accuracy

The output was checked against the verified information, and unsupported operational details were not allowed to be invented.

Iteration Relay

The AI output was improved through multiple versions.

Version 2 — Correct the Facts

Corrected the flight, route, delay duration and departure time.

Version 3 — Remove Unsupported Information

Removed unsupported gate, compensation, refund, meal, hotel and policy information.

Version 4 — Add Passenger Instructions

Added the instruction to monitor official airport display screens.

Version 5 — Improve the Tone

Changed the message to a professional, calm and reassuring tone.

Version 6 — Control the Length

Reduced the announcement to below 80 words.

Final Version — Add Verification Safeguard

Added:

[VERIFY WITH OFFICIAL SYSTEM]

to indicate that missing operational information must be checked through an official system.

Prompt-Refinement Trail

Each follow-up prompt addressed one specific problem:

Version	Problem Addressed	Main Change
Version 1	Initial output	Passenger announcement generated
Version 2	Incorrect/missing details	Corrected flight and delay information
Version 3	Unsupported information	Removed invented operational and policy details
Version 4	Incomplete communication	Added passenger instructions
Version 5	Tone	Made the message professional and reassuring
Version 6	Excessive length	Reduced the announcement below 80 words
Final Version	Risk of guessing	Added verification safeguard




Initial vs Final Output

The final output improved the following areas:

Better factual accuracy
Use of verified information
Removal of unsupported claims
Clear passenger instructions
Professional and reassuring tone
Concise communication
Verification safeguard

Even after refinement, human approval was still required before sending the aviation communication.

Targeted Prompting

The lab demonstrates that targeted follow-up prompts are more useful than vague instructions such as:

Make it better.

A targeted prompt identifies the exact problem and specifies the required correction.

For example, the prompt can request removal of unsupported compensation, replacement of an unverified gate number with [VERIFY GATE], a professional tone and a specific word limit.

Baggage Complaint Scenario

A second iteration challenge focused on a passenger baggage complaint.

Potential unsupported claims included:

Baggage being described as officially lost
24-hour delivery promise
₹10,000 compensation promise
Free upgrade promise

These were removed because they were not verified.

The corrected response used terms such as delayed or untraced baggage and instructed the passenger to verify the baggage reference number through an authorised channel. It also used [VERIFY AIRLINE POLICY] for compensation or delivery timelines.

Boarding-Gate Change Scenario

Another scenario involved a boarding-gate change announcement.

The final communication:

Did not invent a gate number
Used [VERIFY GATE]
Directed passengers to official airport display screens
Used a professional and concise tone
Required human verification before release

Operational details must be verified through the official airport system before the announcement is issued.

Peer Review

The final output was reviewed for:

Supported facts
Removal of unsupported information
Appropriate tone
Clarity and conciseness
Passenger instructions
Verification placeholders
Protection of personal information
Requirement for human approval

Peer feedback helped confirm that the communication was clear, concise, professional and based on verified information.

Key Learnings
Iterative prompting improves AI-generated outputs step-by-step.
The first AI response should always be reviewed.
Targeted prompts are clearer than vague follow-up instructions.
Problems should be addressed one at a time.
Unsupported operational details should never be invented.
Verification placeholders can be used when information is missing.
Compensation and refund information should be checked against applicable airline policy.
Human approval is necessary for important aviation communications.
Personal information should not be entered into public AI tools.
AI Usage Declaration

ChatGPT and/or Google Gemini were used to generate and refine outputs during the activity. The student independently identified problems, created targeted follow-up prompts, reviewed output versions and completed the reflection.

Conclusion

This lab demonstrated that iterative prompting is an effective method for improving Generative AI outputs in aviation management.

By identifying one problem at a time, applying targeted corrections and adding verification safeguards, the final communication became more accurate, concise and professional.

However, AI-generated aviation communication still requires verification and approval by authorised personnel before being released to passengers.
